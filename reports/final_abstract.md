# Final Academic Abstract
## AI-Driven Intelligent Greywater Management and Smart Reuse Routing System

**Keywords:** Greywater Reclamation, Smart Water Routing, Machine Learning, XGBoost, Isolation Forest, Cyber-Physical Systems, Synthetic Digital Twin, Weather Context, Arrhenius Decay Kinetics, TreeSHAP Explainability.

---

### Abstract

Rapid global urbanization and intensifying freshwater scarcity necessitate decentralized, circular water economies. Domestic greywater—originating from bathroom basins, showers, laundry machines, and kitchen sinks—constitutes up to 50–70% of residential wastewater. However, greywater quality fluctuates dramatically across fixtures, human usage patterns, and storage residence times. Conventional reclamation systems rely on static plumbing configurations or indiscriminate binary discharge into municipal sewers, leading to premature membrane fouling, uncontrolled surface runoff during storm events, or hazardous anaerobic microbial regrowth during prolonged tank storage.

This project designs and implements an autonomous, prototype cyber-physical software platform that couples supervised machine learning, unsupervised anomaly screening, deterministic physical safety precedence, meteorological weather context, hydraulic storage decay kinetics, and Shapley additive explanations (TreeSHAP). The baseline dataset comprises 1,500 domestic greywater samples characterized across 14 physicochemical and microbiological parameters ($pH$, turbidity, total suspended solids, dissolved solids, conductivity, dissolved oxygen, biochemical oxygen demand, chemical oxygen demand, ammonium fluoride, nitrate, potassium, and *Escherichia coli*). Because the raw operational target was uninformative (100% "Needs Treatment"), a 4-class operational reuse target was systematically formulated based on international reclamation standards (EPA/WHO): *Class 0 (Sewer Bypass)*, *Class 1 (Bio-filtration)*, *Class 2 (Restricted Irrigation)*, and *Class 3 (Indoor Reuse)*.

An optimized Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) classifier serves as the primary routing model, evaluated against a Random Forest baseline on an independent holdout test partition ($n=225$, 15%) under strict data leakage controls. The optimized XGBoost model achieved 97.78% test accuracy, a Macro F1-score of 0.7346, a Weighted F1-score of 0.9756, and a critical Sewer Bypass recall of 0.9701. To safeguard against out-of-distribution observations, an unsupervised Isolation Forest screens for multivariate novelty without allowing statistical anomalies alone to trigger wasteful sewer diversions. 

A physics-constrained synthetic Digital Twin models stochastic diurnal residential discharge cycles at 15-minute intervals. Dynamic meteorological arbitration ingests Open-Meteo forecasts via a 30-minute in-memory cache, deferring surface irrigation to storage during precipitation events ($> 5.0\text{ mm}$) to prevent nutrient runoff. A dynamic $1000\text{ L}$ storage model couples first-in, first-out (FIFO) hydraulic tracking with temperature-dependent Arrhenius decay kinetics, escalating stagnant water ($> 24-48\text{ h}$, deterioration index $\ge 0.70$) to aeration or operational review. Central arbitration is governed by a 6-tier hierarchical routing engine where deterministic physical safety cutoffs ($pH < 6.0$, acute pathogen spikes, extreme COD) unconditionally override ML predictions and weather conditions. Local instance attributions and global feature rankings are rendered transparently via TreeSHAP, and the entire workflow is operationalized in a 10-page interactive Streamlit dashboard.

**Engineering Limitations:** The supervised routing labels are rule-derived; model accuracy measures operational rule fidelity rather than independent ecological or biological safety validation. Telemetry streams originate from a synthetic simulator rather than physical IoT sensors. Storage decay parameters reflect mechanistic approximations that require empirical laboratory validation prior to physical deployment.
